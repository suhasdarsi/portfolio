## How the market is shaped

- **Identity providers** (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Ping) own login and SSO, and are expanding into everything around it.
- **Governance and privileged access** (SailPoint, Saviynt, CyberArk, BeyondTrust, Delinea) were separate markets for years and are now merging.
- **Newer specialists** cover gaps the platforms are slow on: modern access requests (ConductorOne, Lumos, Opal), identity threat detection (Silverfort, Push Security), and non-human identities (Astrix, Oasis, Aembit).
- **Open source** (Keycloak, authentik, ZITADEL) is a serious option for both workforce and customer login.

## Where the overlap is

Every large identity vendor now claims to cover workforce SSO, governance, privileged access and threat detection. Non-human identities are where the market is moving fastest: service accounts, API keys and the credentials AI agents use already outnumber people, and every vendor is adding coverage for them. This overlaps with secrets management and AI security.

## Consolidation

Palo Alto Networks completed its $25 billion purchase of CyberArk in February 2026, after CyberArk had bought Venafi for machine identities. Delinea bought StrongDM, and SailPoint bought Entro for non-human identities. Okta owns Auth0, and Cisco owns Duo.

## How to buy

SSO with phishing-resistant MFA comes first, and it’s usually tied to Microsoft or Google. For everything else, ask how each vendor handles identities that aren’t people. That is where the next breaches are coming from.
