## How the market is shaped

- **Practitioner tools** (Burp Suite, Nmap, Metasploit, BloodHound, Ghidra, Hashcat) are the standard kit. Most are open source or cheap, and many have been around for over a decade.
- **Commercial red-team frameworks** (Cobalt Strike, alongside open-source Sliver and Mythic) support professional engagements.
- **Automated pentesting** (Pentera, Horizon3.ai NodeZero, XBOW, Pentest-Tools.com) runs attacks continuously instead of once a year.
- **Bug bounty platforms** (HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti) sell access to outside researchers.

## Where the overlap is

Automated pentesting overlaps with vulnerability management and breach-and-attack simulation. All three try to show which weaknesses are actually exploitable. Recon tools (Nuclei, Amass) overlap with attack surface management. AI is changing this category fastest: AI agents now find and exploit real vulnerabilities, and some of them rank highly on bug bounty leaderboards.

## Consolidation

Offensive tooling consolidates less than other categories, because so much of it is open source. Bug bounty and pentest-as-a-service providers are merging with automated testing, as human and AI testing start to sell together.

## How to buy

For defenders, the useful question is whether your controls stop real attacks. Automated pentesting and adversary emulation answer that more often than a yearly pentest report. Only test systems you own, or have written permission to test.
