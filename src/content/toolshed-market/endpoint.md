## How the market is shaped

- **EDR platforms** (CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Microsoft Defender) lead the market and use the endpoint agent as a base to sell identity, cloud, SIEM and AI security.
- **Established antivirus vendors** (Sophos, ESET, Bitdefender, Trellix, Malwarebytes) compete on price and on markets the leaders serve less, such as small businesses and specific regions.
- **Device management** (Microsoft Intune, Jamf, Iru, Fleet, Tanium) is a separate market that increasingly overlaps with security.
- **Open source and free tools** (osquery, Santa, the Objective-See utilities) are widely used, especially on Macs.

## Where the overlap is

EDR vendors are adding IT and device management features, and device management vendors are adding EDR. On Macs especially, one agent often does both. The newest overlap is AI: the endpoint is where coding agents, local MCP servers and AI extensions run, and both EDR vendors and AI security specialists are building visibility for them. EDR sees processes and files. It usually doesn’t understand what an AI agent is permitted to do.

## Consolidation

The endpoint market has been through its main consolidation. Arctic Wolf bought Cylance, and the leaders now grow by adding adjacent products rather than buying endpoint rivals. Kandji rebranded as Iru.

## How to buy

EDR is close to a commodity for basic protection. Choose on detection quality in independent tests, how good the response tools are, and how well it fits your device management. Then check what it can tell you about the AI tools running on each device.
