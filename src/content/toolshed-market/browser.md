## How the market is shaped

Two approaches compete:

- **Enterprise browsers** (Island, Prisma Access Browser, Surf) replace Chrome or Edge with a managed browser. You get deep control, but people have to switch.
- **Extensions** (Keep Aware, SquareX, and the extensions now sold by Akamai and CrowdStrike) add controls to the browser people already use. They are lighter, and nobody has to change habits.

Remote browser isolation, which Menlo Security pioneered, is the older third approach. It is now mostly used for high-risk browsing.

## Where the overlap is

The enterprise-browser vendors now also offer extensions, for users who won’t switch. Browser security also overlaps heavily with SASE, DLP and AI security, because the browser is where data is pasted into AI tools. Many buyers get browser controls as part of a SASE or endpoint contract rather than as a separate purchase.

## Consolidation

Palo Alto Networks bought Talon to build Prisma Access Browser. CrowdStrike bought Seraphic, and Akamai bought LayerX. Island is the largest remaining independent.

## How to buy

Decide first whether you can make people use a different browser. If you can’t, start with an extension. Ask what each product sees of AI use in the browser: pastes, uploads, extensions talking to AI services. That is the fastest-growing reason to buy.
