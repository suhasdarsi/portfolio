Most breaches now start with a stolen identity rather than a software exploit. Identity security covers logging in, what each identity can reach, and spotting when one is misused.

## How it breaks down

- **Single sign-on and identity providers** give one login for every app, with MFA and conditional access.
- **Customer identity** handles sign-up and login for your own product’s users.
- **Access governance** answers who has access to what and why, and handles requests, reviews and removal when people change roles or leave.
- **Privileged access** controls and records access to admin accounts, servers and databases.
- **Identity threat protection** detects attacks on identities themselves, such as stolen sessions, MFA fatigue and Active Directory abuse.
- **Non-human identities** covers the service accounts, API keys and tokens used by machines, which now include AI agents. They usually outnumber people many times over.
- **Passwords and keys** are password managers and hardware keys for individuals.

## Where to start

SSO with phishing-resistant MFA, plus a password manager, covers the most ground. Then reduce standing admin access, and take stock of non-human identities before AI agents multiply them further.
